Door Magnet Lab

Slowing down and latching a barn-style sliding door. Each approach is its own page with its own physics model, kept as it was when explored.

Door80 × 200 cm, about 12 kg
Railsteel flat bar, face 40 mm tall
Hanger strapsteel, 30 mm wide, 5 mm thick
Strap to rail15 mm (a little less usable)
Bumpersrubber, Ø25 × 15 mm, centre screw
Magnets ownedØ15 × 5 mm, cheap (≈ N35)

Approaches

1. Magnets: eddy-current brake + magnetic latch

Explored 2026-11-21 → 22 · copper strip on the rail, magnets on the hangers, magnet in the bumper
  • Latch works: magnet in the bumper + magnet on the hanger + dead bumper closes and holds from 0.3 to 1.2 m/s, about 35 N to open.
  • Slow-down is weak: the brake takes 0.1–0.2 m/s off a push, and a stronger brake makes gentle closes stop short of the latch.
  • Side pull: brake magnets pull the hanger onto the steel rail along the whole travel (57–169 N).

2. Two-sided rail brake + pull-in spring

Explored 2026-11-22 · blocked · 30 × 40 × 10 blocks on both sides of the rail, copper 40 × 2 mm on both faces, constant-force spring over the last 250 mm, latch from approach 1
  • Soft-close works in the model: every push from 0.2 to 2.0 m/s ends closed and held; gentle pushes arrive at about 0.2 m/s, a 0.8 m/s push at 0.25–0.36 m/s.
  • Blocked: the fixtures holding the rail to the wall are in the way of the back magnets (confirmed 2026-11-22).
  • Kept because it shows what works: a strong brake plus a long constant-force spring makes a real soft-close.
